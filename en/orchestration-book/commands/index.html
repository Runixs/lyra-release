Command reference

Only the form each job uses. For full usage, add --help to a command or run lyra skills get lyra-cli.

Start

Section titled “Start”

Start a fresh Worker: --spec-file creates a task; --task runs an existing ready task under its current owner. A new Workspace needs a checkout on the destination host: use lyra project clone with --host and --project, or register an existing checkout with lyra project location add, before lyra worker start --host. With a remote URL, the agent proceeds without asking. In Windows Git Bash, apply MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1 only to the command passing remote POSIX paths. A Worker closes once its report is acknowledged; --keep-open keeps it for reuse.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — retry failed or stopped tasks first (task retry). Run --task from the current owner’s Lyra session. If a step stops, follow error.detail.next.

Starts a Lead for one part, in its own Workspace.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — refused when its children would have no room.

Opens a new tree with no parent. Settings decide who may.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead — a person passes --workspace and --agent.

Open a separate top-level Orchestrator session. An agent can dispatch into it later unless a detach or move takes it out of the agent’s tree. Use worker start --task when a fresh session should be the task owner’s child Worker.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead

Wait

Section titled “Wait”

One wait returns messages, held outgoing mail, overdue tasks and Worker conditions. Act on the messages, then explicitly acknowledge their delivery before waiting again. --until-ended does not wake per report: it returns once, with all reports, when every task you own has ended.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — a Claude Code Lead runs it in the background like an Orchestrator and ends its turn; an agent without background commands waits in the foreground.

Claims and prints one message batch, waiting up to 60 seconds. Explicitly acknowledge it after acting; until then the batch replays.

Who runs it: Worker — never run it beside task wait.

Shows that session’s turn and status.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead

Reports and results

Section titled “Reports and results”

Shows status, outcome and summary without the spec.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead · Worker

Lists the tasks of your plan; after a compaction or a restart, start again from here.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead

Shows the changed files in that Workspace.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead

Reads what that session did and said: the transcript of Claude Code, or the terminal screen of another agent. Sends it nothing.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead

Reports once, ending the attempt; the summary puts the result first.

Who runs it: Lead · Worker — Workers use task report for their own attempt. A task owner can settle a closed Worker’s task with lyra task settle <task-id> --outcome completed --reason "<result and evidence>".

Step in

Section titled “Step in”

Makes a failed or stopped task ready again; fix the cause first.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — --abandon-unproven is person-only.

Irreversibly ends the agent process; its attempt fails as stopped by force and the task stays with its owner.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead — a person: any session. Orchestrator or Lead: only sessions below it. Worker: none. Exit 3 requires inspection, never a duplicate task.

Types one Ctrl-C: a request. The stop is proven by a report or the process ending.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead — --yes only on the person’s word; --abandon-unproven is person-only.

Takes a ready, failed or stopped task out of the plan and blocks its dependents for good.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — --yes only on the person’s word.

Takes over an ended session’s plan, tasks and ordinary mail; later sends to its old address follow the adopter.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — only on the person’s word.

Tasks by hand

Section titled “Tasks by hand”

Creates a task in a staged plan; --depends-on only for real order.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead

Move a task deadline or lower its failed-attempt cap. Only a person may raise the cap.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead

Lists only what can start now; a completed task starts nothing by itself.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead

Gives a task to an existing session. It is refused only when a detach or move took the session out of the agent’s tree; detach and move also end old custody, including finished tasks.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — accepted means it has the task, not that it finished.

Decision gates

Section titled “Decision gates”

Holds a task and what follows until the person chooses.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — after exit 3, read request show and gate list; retry with the same request ID and arguments to get the original receipt.

Lists the gates on that task.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead

Records the person’s choice, exactly as one of the options.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — time passing is not a choice.

Handoff

Section titled “Handoff”

Passes one task’s ownership after the receiver explicitly acknowledges the delivery. Claiming or printing alone does not transfer ownership.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead

Abandons a handoff whose receiver is closed.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — only when the receiver is confirmed closed, on the person’s word.

Messages

Section titled “Messages”

Ask and wait for a paired answer; resume the same question after timeout.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead · Worker — default 10m, maximum 30m. The question ID is printed before the wait and kept after a timeout; --list finds your questions again.

Answer a question once, from its recipient session.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead · Worker — use the question ID shown by message check; the asker receives the paired answer.

Sends ordinary mail; accepted does not mean read.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead · Worker — a message never approves anything.

Shows the delivery and the doorbell (delivery.pointer).

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead · Worker

Reads a thread without receiving it.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead · Worker

Reuse and release

Section titled “Reuse and release”

Gives a still-open finished Worker its next task, keeping context. Prompt rights or current task custody are required; old terminal custody fenced by detach or move cannot be reused through starter prompt rights.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead

Count finished Workers kept for reuse with terminal list --terminal-state reclaimable and tell the person how many remain. lyra terminal release frees a selected terminal when they ask.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead — a finished Worker closes by itself once its report is acknowledged. Release one kept with --keep-open only on the person’s request, or with their agreement when reuse is impossible; a listed release command is not permission.

Check

Section titled “Check”

Shows this computer’s lyra and skills versions.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead · Worker

Reads only the reference your next step needs.

Who runs it: Orchestrator · Lead · Worker

Shows whether lyra-daemon on this computer is running.

Who runs it: a person · Orchestrator · Lead · Worker
